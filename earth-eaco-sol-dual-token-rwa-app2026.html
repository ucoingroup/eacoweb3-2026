01 · Logic Correction

Why ERC-3643 Does Not Apply to EACO

EACO runs natively on Solana. Some compliance write-ups borrow EVM notions like ERC-3643 or Solidity transfer hooks - they do not exist in EACO's technical stack. The table below is the correction every reader should keep.

#EVM write-up (wrong for EACO)Why it is wrong
1 "Use an ERC-3643 compliant token instead of a fully open ERC-20." ERC-3643 is an Ethereum (EVM) tokenisation standard; EACO runs on Solana (SPL Token Program / Token-2022) with no EVM contract stack and no "ERC" standard.
2 function _beforeTokenTransfer(...) // Solidity hook _beforeTokenTransfer is an OpenZeppelin ERC extension point; Solana programs have no such hook. The equivalent is the SPL Token-2022 transfer hook (CPI from the token program) or an in-program whitelist account.

The correct mental model: three-layer separation

INCENTIVE

Incentive / circulation layer - EACO (SPL Token)

Green-power rewards · EnergyNFT certificates · ecosystem rights · community circulation

LEDGER

Accounting & verification layer - Solar Ledger

Plant onboarding · oracle signatures · CO₂ accounting · audit snapshots

SOLANA

Chain & settlement layer - Solana

Low-fee high-frequency micro-ledger · throughput · account rent model

EVM world: ERC-3643 = compliant token standard (ONCHAINID + whitelist + token contract in one).
Solana world: the same capability is delivered jointly by chain (settlement) + program (accounting / whitelist) + token standard (SPL / Token-2022) - no single contract carries all compliance semantics.
Therefore this page's compliance checklist and code examples all use the Solana framing; ERC-3643 appears only as an EVM mirror note (see §9.4).

07 · Global Compliance & Solana Developer

US · EU · CN Frameworks & Solana Compliance

Compliance is the first life-or-death line for RWA. A crypto-native asset can launch first and be audited later - but an RWA is tied to real assets and fiat value. Compliance is not optional; it is a threshold from day one. Cross a red line and the result is not a bug in the code, but frozen custody accounts, delisted products and real legal liability for the team. So the first step of building an RWA is not writing a contract - it is understanding which market, sold to whom, and under which supervision.

🇺🇸

United States · GENIUS Act

The US approach is classification: the GENIUS Act splits crypto assets into three classes.

  • Digital commodities — purely decentralised assets like BTC and ETH sit under commodity rules, relatively light.
  • Securities — the home of tokenised stocks and bonds; SEC oversight, specific exemptions, usually limited to qualified investors.
  • Payment stablecoins — a new class with 1:1 reserve and segregated custody, co-regulated at state and federal level.

Impact: tokenised Treasuries are securities for qualified investors only - a narrow door, but behind it sits the largest capital pool.

🇪🇺

European Union · MiCA

MiCA, the world's first complete crypto framework, fully applies in 2026 - the key word is recognition.

  • Asset-Referenced Tokens (ART) — a clear registration and licensing path for tokenised assets.
  • E-Money Tokens (EMT) — legal status of on-chain assets written into law, no longer a grey zone.
  • One passport for all of Europe — register once in one member state, operate across the EU, with relatively flexible retail access under limits.

Impact: smoother than per-product security review; the EU is the preferred compliant testbed for many RWA projects.

🇨🇳

China · Eight-Ministry framework

In February 2026, eight ministries jointly issued a policy that for the first time allows domestic assets to be tokenised and issued offshore.

  • Offshore issuance of domestic assets — quality assets such as infrastructure and supply-chain finance raise funds for offshore investors through compliant channels.
  • No domestic speculation — domestic financial institutions must not directly trade crypto.
  • No domestic sales — RWA must not be sold to domestic residents; FX and AML rules apply strictly.

Impact: different inside and out - no domestic speculation, but blockchain financing of real assets is supported, with issuance and investors offshore.

Which market should you choose?

🇺🇸US — institutional scale

Highest bar and heaviest compliance, but the largest pool and closest to traditional finance.

🇪🇺EU — compliance certainty

The clearest registration path; one licence covers all of Europe.

🇨🇳China — offshore issuance

Policy-supported corridor for teams holding Chinese real assets.

Developer compliance checklist (Solana framing)

In line with EACO's on-chain facts, this checklist is written for the Solana / SPL stack - the EVM wording of the old draft is retired except as the mirror note below.

🔐

SPL whitelist token (transfer hook)

EACO is a Solana SPL token. Compliance whitelisting uses the SPL Token-2022 transfer hook (the token program CPI-calls a hook program on every transfer) or an in-program whitelist account - not a fully open SPL transfer. Whitelist entries are written by KYC / identity flows.

📊

NAV pricing

Daily NAV snapshots or authoritative sources (e.g. the OrbMarkets public data baseline) - never on-chain AMM discovery; ledger/credential pricing follows official snapshots.

🌍

Geo-blocking

Restrict interaction from non-cleared jurisdictions - checks happen at registration and at whitelist write time, so uncleared jurisdictions never enter the whitelist and cannot receive transfers or participate in rights.

🪪

On-chain identity

Integrate on-chain identity verification (regulated KYC results bound to addresses) so holders stay traceable and regulator-recognised; identity and whitelist entries are one-to-one.

🔗

The relationship of the four: identity verification (4) produces the "compliant conclusion" → whitelist (1) fixes the "may receive transfers" right → geo-blocking (3) intercepts non-cleared jurisdictions before the whitelist write → NAV (2) prices without fabrication. The whitelist and identity are the on-chain landing points of off-chain compliance: code fixes already-granted permissions, it does not create them.

Solana whitelist transfer check - only whitelisted addresses can receive (illustrative)transfer_hook.rs
// Illustrative snippet for design patterns only - NOT deployable as-is.
// Real implementation needs an identity registration program, governance
// and issuance logic, plus the compliance sales permit obtained first.

#[derive(Accounts)]
pub struct ExecuteTransferHook<'info> {
    /// CHECK: Token-2022 program passes transfer context via CPI
    #[account(has_one = mint)]
    pub token_program: AccountInfo<'info>, // SPL Token-2022
    #[account(mut)]
    pub source: AccountInfo<'info>,        // sender token account
    #[account(mut)]
    pub destination: AccountInfo<'info>,   // receiver token account
    pub mint: AccountInfo<'info>,          // EACO mint
    pub authority: Signer<'info>,
    /// Whitelist state (in-program whitelist account, written by KYC flow)
    #[account(seeds = [b"whitelist", mint.key().as_ref()], bump)]
    pub whitelist: Account<'info, WhitelistState>,
}

pub fn transfer_hook(
    ctx: Context<ExecuteTransferHook>,
    _amount: u64,
) -> Result<()> {
    let source_key = ctx.accounts.source.key();
    let destination_key = ctx.accounts.destination.key();
    let mint_key = ctx.accounts.mint.key();

    // Skip mint (source == mint) and burn (destination == mint)
    if source_key == mint_key || destination_key == mint_key {
        return Ok(());
    }

    // Only whitelisted addresses can receive transfers
    require!(
        ctx.accounts.whitelist.is_verified(&destination_key),
        TransferHookError::BuyerNotVerified
    );
    Ok(())
}

The program encodes compliance permissions already granted; it does not create them. WhitelistState (PDA, seeds = ["whitelist", mint]): bump / initialized / entries: map<Pubkey, WhitelistEntry>. WhitelistEntry: holder, verified_at (reviewable), jurisdiction (for geo-blocking).

EVM mirror note (only if the project mirrors to an EVM chain): ERC-3643 performs the same check through the _beforeTokenTransfer hook - require(_isVerified[to], "buyer not verified"). Solana has no such hook; the equivalent constraint lives in the transfer hook / in-program whitelist accounts. Only a future EVM mirror of EACO would need the ERC-3643 framing.

09 · Engineering Details

Single-file, zero-dependency web app

Everything a developer needs to know about the shape of this page, the i18n mechanism, the theming hook and the data model that maps to a real on-chain ledger.

Technical form

Single-file self-contained: HTML + CSS + JS and the six-language i18n dictionaries are all inlined - zero external dependencies, zero external resources, works offline when opened directly.

  • Vanilla JS (ES5-style IIFE + 'use strict'), no framework, no build step
  • Inline SVG charts (no chart library)

i18n mechanism

Six languages: en (default) / zh / es / ar / fr / ru; Arabic auto-switches to RTL, all others LTR. Around 300 keys × 6 languages with zero missing keys (Week 41 state).

  • Binding: data-i18n for text, data-ph for placeholders; applyI18n() replaces textContent
  • Fallback: tr(key) falls back to I18N.en[key] so a missing key never blanks the UI
  • Language switch re-renders the table and the SVG chart

Theme mechanism

html[data-theme] = solar | ocean | forest; three sets of CSS variables (--bg, --text, --muted, --accent, --line, --card, ...). setTheme(t) flips the attribute and the active button; SVG charts read var(--accent) and follow automatically.

  • Theme and language switch instantly within the session (not persisted)
  • The only state is the in-memory AI checklist

Data model (real on-chain mapping)

SolarPlant: id / name / location / capacity_MW / output_MWh / co2_avoided_t (output × 0.5) / status (running | grid) / deviceSN + encrypted_chip / oracle_signature. WhitelistState/WhitelistEntry: holder / verified_at / jurisdiction.

  • cURL-like semantics: unsigned entries rejected
  • NAV from official snapshots, never AMM discovery

Testability design (CI door for weekly checks)

Syntax — node --check on the extracted script
Key parity — six language key sets are equal (zero missing, zero extra)
Binding completeness — every data-i18n / data-ph key exists in the dictionary
Runtime & responsive — headless-browser assertions: 3 themes, 6 languages, RTL, CRUD, chart, toast; 375 px no horizontal overflow
Neutrality — keyword scan for investment guidance / no trading or liquidity-pool promotion

FAQ

Q1Why does the FAQ say 128 plants but the ledger shows 10?
128 is the conceptual demo number on the hero stat card; the 10 records are in-memory seed data you can add to, filter and delete. Both are for demonstration.
Q2EACO and SOL - who is on-chain?
Both are on Solana. SOL carries the chain, gas, custody and settlement; EACO carries ecosystem incentives and circulation.
Q3Can EACO use ERC-3643?
Not directly. ERC-3643 is an Ethereum (EVM) tokenised-RWA standard built on ONCHAINID and ERC-20 extension points; EACO is a Solana SPL token whose equivalent controls are SPL Token-2022 transfer hook / in-program whitelist accounts. ERC-3643 framing is only relevant for a future EVM mirror.
Q4Why ≥ 10 kWh per NFT?
To avoid fragmented minting and define EnergyNFT as a verifiable green-power production certificate rather than an arbitrary measurement unit.
Q5Where do I verify EACO's on-chain data and price?
OrbMarkets token page is the baseline (Price / Holders / 24h Volume / Supply / Market Cap / FDV). SOL live price via CoinGecko; EACO without a public API falls back to page collection or static values.
Q6Can the 9.4 Solana snippet be deployed?
No - it is an illustrative pattern showing how "only whitelisted addresses can receive" works with SPL Token-2022 transfer hook or an in-program whitelist. Real deployment needs identity registration, governance, issuance logic and a compliance sales permit first.

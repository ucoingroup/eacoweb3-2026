## 1. What the SEC announced on August 18

On Tuesday, August 18, 2026, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) proposed **"Regulation Crypto Assets" ("Reg Crypto")** — a tailored offering framework for certain investment contracts involving crypto assets.

**Context:**
- Originally scheduled for an Aug 14 open meeting, postponed at the last minute due to a scheduling conflict; the proposal was released Aug 18.
- A flagship output of Chair **Paul Atkins'** "Project Crypto" initiative.
- Follows the SEC's **March 2026 interpretation** of how federal securities laws apply to digital assets — moving from guidance to formal rulemaking.
- Runs in parallel with the **CLARITY Act**, which stalled in the Senate earlier this month; the SEC is using its existing authority to move first.

**Atkins:** the proposal "seeks to provide crypto asset entrepreneurs and market participants with clear pathways to raise capital under the federal securities laws."

**Four key mechanisms:**

1. **Tier 1 exemption**: up to **$5 million over 4 years** without traditional securities registration;
2. **Tier 2 exemption**: up to **$75 million in any 12-month period**, requiring financial statements + ongoing reporting;
3. **Conditional safe harbor**: a token tied to an investment contract can shed that status once the issuer completes or abandons its promised "essential managerial efforts" — echoing Commissioner **Peirce's 2020/2021 token safe harbors**;
4. **State preemption**: registration/qualification requirements are preempted for covered offerings and qualifying secondary-market transactions — one federal regime instead of 50.

**Other key points:**
- Both tiers require **principles-based narrative disclosures** (a crypto-adapted whitepaper + risk disclosure) instead of a full traditional prospectus.
- The proposal opens a **60-day public comment period** after publication in the Federal Register.
- The SEC may revise the proposal before finalizing; there is no statutory deadline.
- Explicit goal: reduce the incentive for crypto firms to raise money offshore and keep projects in the U.S.

## 2. Basis for the $5M / $75M figures

**These two figures come directly from the SEC proposal itself** — two exemption lanes designed for crypto issuers:

| Lane | Cap | Period | Extra requirements | Benchmark |
|------|-----|--------|--------------------|-----------|
| Exemption 1 (Tier 1) | **$5M** | **4 years** | principles-based narrative disclosures | lightweight lane for early-stage projects |
| Exemption 2 (Tier 2) | **$75M** | **12 months** | financial statements + ongoing reporting | matches **Regulation A Tier 2** cap |

**Why these numbers:**
- **$5M / 4 years**: a light-touch lane for early-stage and community tokens, minimizing compliance cost barriers.
- **$75M / 12 months**: identical to the existing **Regulation A Tier 2** ceiling — the SEC deliberately aligned with this mature "mini-IPO" standard (Reg A Tier 2 allows up to $75M in 12 months with audited financials + ongoing disclosure). This signals crypto fundraising is being wired into the existing U.S. capital-markets system.
- Both lanes share **principles-based narrative disclosures**, replacing heavy prospectus burdens with crypto-adapted disclosure.
- **Sources**: SEC official press release of 2026-08-18 (posted by @SECGov), confirmed by Barron's / WSJ, crypto.news, edgeX and other outlets.
